Flight from the Dark: The Playthrough Diaries

Three attempts, one arbitrary death, and a bone comb.

Make Your Choice
18 April 2026 · 8 min read

This is the first of (hopefully) many entries on this blog in which I will detail what actually happened in my playthroughs of certain gamebooks, starting with Flight from the Dark, the first book in the Lone Wolf series. If you're curious to know how and why I failed, this is for you.

Before I begin, some quick notes: to save time, I used a d10 for this rather than the table at the back of the book (with 10 = 0). And, I hope this goes without saying, this entire page is filled with spoilers. You have been warned.

Attempt #1: Going in Blind

I began my first playthrough excited and eager. For this run, I had some decent dice rolls to begin with: I ended up with 16 Combat Skill and 28 Endurance. Based on this, I decided to lean into the "defense-first" playstyle and simply seek to outlive any opponents I face. For my disciplines, I chose Hunting (not having to deal with the periodic -3 to endurance seemed like a good idea), Healing (again, leaning into endurance), Weaponskill: sword (because I rolled an 8), Sixth Sense (danger avoided is endurance points saved), and Mindshield (pure defence).

And so off I went. I soon came to the Ruins of Raumas and, as the heroic defender of all, I happily leapt into my first encounter against the Giak about to attack the Mage. The difference between our Combat Skills was massive and I rolled really well, so I one-shot him and took no damage. Perfect start.

Soon afterwards I found myself climbing a hill and then hiding in a cave. The dice gods were on my side once again, and I rolled high enough to dodge the Giaks pursuing me. I then found myself climbing into a tree house, where a kind hermit gave me a warhammer, buffing my combat skill even further. How could I possibly be defeated?

I found myself in yet another cave, this time against a Burrowcrawler. Gross. I killed it in a single round too. I was steamrolling through this and feeling really confident. It wasn't long before I encountered a caravan of refugees, who I joined. They were then attacked by more monsters, and I once again leapt to the defence of the defenceless. I killed the attacking Giaks in two turns, losing only two endurance points. Not great, but I was still a force to be reckoned with. Fear me, monsters.

After investigating a farmhouse, I met soldiers who were part of the elite royal guard, led by Prince Pelethar, the son of the king, who was under attack. After a series of heroics from my character, I found myself face-to-face with the massive monster that the Prince had just been fighting: the Gourgaz. More endurance than me, the same combat skill (thanks to the cool sword I had just received from the Prince). This one was going to be tough, but I was ready.

The fight was epic. The first few turns were rough: low dice rolls meant that our endurance pools were going down at a similar rate. I needed some luck from the dice gods, and just as I thought I was about to die... I rolled an 8, and then a 9! With just 6 endurance points left, I had slain the mighty Gourgaz. It felt good. Sadly Prince Pelethar died soon thereafter, but I was happy to have proven myself. Now there could be no question that I was a loyal, brave Kai warrior. Hopefully the other soldiers would remember my bravery, and I would be rewarded for it later on. I was falling in love with this book.

I was given a horse and departed. I then took a right turn, got shot by an arrow, fell into a hunting pit and died.

Yep. Just like that. No warning, no indication. Just an arbitrary death. The cardinal sin of gamebooks, for me at least, is the arbitrary death. I hate them. I had thoroughly enjoyed the book up until that point, so I wasn't going to let it kill my enthusiasm, but it did leave a sour taste in my mouth.

Attempt #2: The Way of the Coward

I had planned to do my second run the following day, but a combination of annoyance and curiosity brought me back just an hour after finishing attempt #1. For the first playthrough of any book I read, I always try to play as a "good" character, and I try to engage with everything. I explore every cave, help every character in need, and fight every enemy. This seemed to be paying off during my first attempt... until it wasn't.

This time, however, I decided to try something completely different. I would avoid every encounter, attempt to escape any fight, and ignore everyone and everything. What I like to call "The Way of the Coward."

I rolled well initially, getting 18 Combat Skill and 25 Endurance. Because I was planning to avoid everything that wasn't essential, I took the disciplines of Camouflage and Tracking, as well as Healing, Sixth Sense, and Hunting. I began by ignoring the young Mage from my first attempt (I assume he died), kept going, avoided caves, stuck to the trees, avoided the refugees, took a canoe down a river, and lost my backpack during the run. Oh, and I ignored suffering soldiers on two occasions as well. I could have helped, but I didn't. I only got myself into one combat situation, against a Giak and Doomwolf, and defeated them in three turns. By the time I got to the capital, it felt less like I was walking there and more that I was staggering over a finish line.

But I did cross it. I met the King, told him what happened at the Kai Monastery, met the girl I assume will be my love interest in future books, and received my quest to proceed to the next installment in the series. The thing is... it felt so hollow. So unsatisfying. These books are fun when you engage with and explore the world. And I was disappointed that I was able to finish it so quickly and so easily.

Of course, I didn't plan on this being my canon run. I was going to play again the following day, and actually play as I usually would. Hopefully avoiding any arbitrary deaths along the way.

Attempt #3: The Canon Run

This was to be it. I felt like I understood the mechanics and style of Flight from the Dark well enough that I could navigate this run successfully.

I rolled a 15 for Combat Skill (bang average) and a 27 Endurance. Not great, but not terrible. I chose the same disciplines as the previous run: Hunting, Healing, Camouflage, Tracking, and Sixth Sense. I wanted to make sure I did everything to avoid any arbitrary deaths.

I encountered the young Mage, and this time I decided to help him by throwing a rock at the head of the attacking Giak. Success — I didn't even need to fight. My aim did the job. We met, shook hands, and departed.

Much of this run was familiar from attempt #1, but I did stumble upon a new village which I investigated. Sadly, all the villagers had been killed, but I managed to loot a Giak Spear to add to my weapons. Soon after, I encountered a mysterious figure standing in the middle of the path, with a raven perched on its extended arm. This is where I was praying that employing logic would prove beneficial. Everyone in the nearby village was dead, killed by the Giaks. So why would someone innocent be standing in the open like this? My danger sense was tingling, and so I chose the option to run and attack the figure. This began my fight with a Vordak.

The fight was tough, lasting five turns, but a couple of high rolls meant that I came out of it with eighteen endurance remaining. My healing discipline would hopefully get me back up to full health in time. The creature was also kind enough to drop a valuable gem, although picking it up cost me an extra two endurance.

I soon encountered the cave from my first run, the one with the Burrowcrawler in it. I entered, fought it (still gross), and managed to one-shot the poor creature thanks to its low endurance. I grabbed the twenty gold coins and departed.

I was ever so slightly disappointed that much of this run resembled my first. I tried taking different choices, but always seemed to end up in familiar places — a reminder that the book's branching is wider in places than it first appears, but the paths converge more than you'd hope. I joined up with the refugees, successfully protected the children, ran to the farmhouse, and met the Elite Guards. This time, however, I chose not to follow them back to meet Prince Pelethar. I was still recovering from my fight with the Vordak and knew I couldn't possibly defeat the Gourgaz waiting for me. The differential in our Combat Skills was simply too great.

Eventually I made my way to the outskirts of the capital, with the choice of taking the road, the river, or trekking through the creepy graveyard. I decided to take my chances on the main road. After looting an abandoned cottage and picking up a variety of knick-knacks, I found myself face-to-face with three bandits. In total, they had north of sixty endurance, and I would have to fight them one at a time. Basically two Gourgazes.

Thankfully, I was given an option to escape this all-but-impossible fight, which I took. They shot me in the back, I collapsed, and eventually found my way into the care of the King's Physician. All my endurance was restored, and I successfully finished the book.
